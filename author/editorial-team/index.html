● Editorial Team

The people behind WealthGuides

We present ourselves as an editorial team rather than a single "expert" author, because that's a more accurate reflection of how the site is actually produced — and because we'd rather be transparent about our process than claim credentials we don't hold.

Research & Data
Editorial Role

Sources and verifies figures directly from SEC filings, fund prospectuses, IRS publications, and FRED before any article is drafted.

Writing
Editorial Role

Translates research into plain-English guides, with a standing rule: if a beginner couldn't follow a paragraph, it gets rewritten.

Fact-Checking & Review
Editorial Role

Independently verifies every statistic against its original source and reviews articles before and after publication for accuracy.

A note on credentials

We don't publish invented certifications, licenses, or employment histories. Where an article draws on a specific professional standard (for example, IRS contribution limits or SEC disclosure rules), we cite the primary source directly rather than asking you to take a byline's word for it. This is a deliberate choice, explained further in our editorial policy.

How this team actually works together

In practice, these three roles aren't three different people locked into rigid lanes on every article — on a small publication, the same researcher may draft a section, and the same editor may double-check a source. What stays consistent regardless of who touches a given article is the sequence: primary-source research happens before a word is drafted, a second pass checks every figure against its original source before publication, and nothing goes live without that fact-check step having occurred. That sequence, not a specific person's title, is what we're actually asking you to trust.

Why we chose this model over a single "expert" byline

Many finance sites lead with a single named expert — often with a professional designation attached to build immediate credibility. We considered that model and chose not to use it, for a specific reason: WealthGuides doesn't currently employ a licensed financial advisor, CFP, or CFA on staff, and inventing one (or implying a credential that doesn't exist) would be a form of the exact misrepresentation we're asking readers to be skeptical of elsewhere online. A transparent editorial-team model, with citations doing the credibility work instead of a personal title, is the more honest option available to a publication at our current stage — and we think it holds up better under scrutiny, not worse.

How to reach the team

Factual corrections, source disputes, or general questions about how a specific article was researched can be sent to corrections@wealthguides.online or through our contact page. We read every message personally — there is no larger organization behind this address routing it elsewhere.
